## 1. Foundations

> **Transformation begins with clarity.**

Sonderence designs digital ecosystems around the way a business actually works. It is an embedded partner for organizations ready to move beyond disconnected processes, fragmented systems, and tools that do not create value.

**Mission**
Sonderence helps businesses transform how they operate by thoughtfully integrating modern technology into every layer of the organization — becoming more efficient, informed, scalable, and resilient not by adding more software, but by designing digital ecosystems that support how the business actually works.

**Vision**
A future where organizations are digitally fluent and technologically competent — not because they use AI or modern software, but because they understand when technology creates value, when it introduces unnecessary complexity, and how to integrate it intentionally. Sonderence's long-term ambition is to be the trusted partner that helps organizations continuously adapt to technological change while maintaining operational excellence.

**Who we work with**
Businesses that have outgrown disconnected processes, fragmented systems, and outdated ways of operating — organizations ready to rethink how they function, not just adopt another tool.

**Positioning — what makes Sonderence different**
- Many firms begin with technology. Sonderence begins with the business.
- Many firms sell software. Sonderence designs operating models.
- Many firms build automations. Sonderence eliminates constraints.
- Many firms focus on implementation. Sonderence focuses on transformation.

### Guiding idea: A calm intelligence.

| | Principle | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Begin with the business** | Understand the operating reality before recommending the technology. |
| 02 | **Design for the whole system** | Technology choices should resolve constraints, not introduce new ones. |
| 03 | **Make the complex legible** | Use evidence, precision, and restraint to create forward motion. |

### Brand personality

| Trait | Description |
|---|---|
| **Partnership-driven & embedded** | Sonderence works as an embedded partner, not an outside consultant delivering a report — deeply understanding each client's operations, culture, and goals over a long-term relationship. |
| **Evidence-based & measured** | Recommendations are informed by observation, experimentation, and measurement — valuing evidence over intuition, and willing to adjust when new information emerges. |
| **Strategic & analytical** | Every technology decision is filtered through systems thinking and first-principles reasoning — understanding the whole business before optimizing any one part of it. |
| **Precise & minimal** | Technology is never added because it's new or exciting — only when it removes a real constraint. The brand should feel deliberate and uncluttered, not maximalist or trend-driven. |

## 2. The Mark

> **Connected by design.**

An abstract "S" formed from two interlocking elements: individual systems resolving into one coherent whole.

**Clear space**
Protect the mark with clear space equal to its own height on every side.

**Minimum size**
Never reproduce below 24px in digital contexts, or 0.25″ in print.

**Misuse**
- Don't stretch, skew, or distort the proportions of the mark.
- Don't recolor the mark outside the approved palette (Section 3).
- Don't place the mark on low-contrast or busy backgrounds.
- Don't add drop shadows, outlines, or other effects not shown here.

## 3. Colour System

> **One hue. Four measures.**

A monochromatic blue system keeps expression focused, technical, and unmistakably Sonderence.

| Swatch | Name | HEX | RGB | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🟦 | **Sonderence Navy** | `#101040` | 16, 16, 64 | Identity, headlines & decisive actions |
| 🔹 | **Sonderence Slate** | `#7C7C96` | 124, 124, 150 | Supporting UI, secondary signals |
| ◽ | **Sonderence Silver** | `#DBDBE2` | 219, 219, 226 | Dividers, quiet surfaces & diagrams |
| ⬜ | **White** | `#FFFFFF` | 255, 255, 255 | Primary ground & negative space |

Navy carries identity and decisive moments (headlines, primary actions). Slate handles supporting UI and secondary signals. Silver stays quiet — dividers, surfaces, diagram fills. White remains the dominant ground. The system deliberately stays within one hue rather than introducing new colors, so nothing competes with Navy for attention.

## 4. Typography

> **Structured, never sterile. Make complexity legible.**

| Role | Typeface | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Headlines** | Onest 600 | Semibold · tight tracking |
| **Body & Interface** | Onest 400 | Regular · generous leading |

A single type family across two weights keeps the system disciplined rather than decorative — tight tracking gives headlines their confidence, while generous leading keeps dense body and interface copy easy to scan.

*Note: this supersedes the earlier Söhne/Inter proposal — Onest is the typeface actually built into the live brand system and is now the confirmed standard.*

## 5. Components

> **Quietly decisive.**

Components use clear hierarchy, structural lines, and a single action colour.

**Editorial / perspective card pattern**
Use this pattern for services, points of view, insights, and proof — the pattern for statements the brand wants to make a claim with, like:

- *"Design the system, not just the solution."* → paired with a text action (e.g., "Start a conversation")
- *"Technology earns its place when it removes a constraint."* → paired with a "Read perspective" link and item counter (e.g., 01 / 04)

The claim does the visual work — minimal decoration, generous whitespace, one clear next step.

## 6. Interface Inventory

> **The working set.**

A flexible range of foundations for websites, product touchpoints, and proposal materials.

**Actions**
- **Primary action** — solid Navy fill, white text (e.g., "Primary action")
- **Secondary action** — outlined, Navy text on white
- **Text action** — underlined link, no container (e.g., "Text action →")

**Field treatment**
- Label above field (e.g., "Work email"), placeholder text in a muted tone (e.g., "name@company.com"), bottom-border-only input — no boxed fields.

**Tags & status**
- Outline tag for neutral labels (e.g., "Systems Strategy")
- Silver fill for descriptive tags (e.g., "Embedded Partner")
- Navy fill for active/status tags (e.g., "In Progress")

**Navigation**
- Bold for current/primary items, muted grey for secondary items, arrow accent for external or "read more" links.

## 7. Graphic Language

> **Systems, made visible.**

Use these graphic patterns as backgrounds, section dividers, data diagrams, or motion starting points.

| Pattern | Description |
|---|---|
| **Interlock** | Overlapping paths (concentric rings in Navy, Slate, and Silver) show connection without clutter. |
| **Network signal** | Fine lines, nodes, and measured relationships — a solid path resolving out of a dotted one, anchored by dots at key points. |
| **Modular grid** | Offset Navy/Slate/Silver/white blocks — a quiet structure for maps, diagrams, and data. |

---

## 8. Brand Expression

> **Interconnected forms. Measured motion.**

Avoid stock imagery and decorative excess. Instead, use spare diagrams, ruled grids, and interconnected forms that clarify relationships. Motion should connect, resolve, or reconfigure — never distract.

This applies across every touchpoint in scope for this guide: web, social media, and generated visual assets (icons, secondary marks, animated graphics). Any AI-generated asset should draw from the Interlock, Network Signal, or Modular Grid patterns above, stay within the four-color palette (Section 3), and set type in Onest (Section 4) — so generated work reads as clearly of the same system rather than a one-off.
